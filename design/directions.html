Imperial Map — four design directions

Each tile below is a mini-mock of the real page (masthead, map, standings, week control, ticker, and how a territory change gets announced) using actual Postseason Week 1 data. The gray chrome around the tiles is just this gallery — everything inside a tile is the direction.

DIRECTION 01

Tecmo Saturday

Full commitment to what the banner already started: the whole site is an NES sports cartridge. The map is the playfield, leaderboards are the post-game stats screen, the week selector is a START-menu control. Hard-step shadows, 4px chrome borders, scanlines, sprite-step motion only.

Signature: every panel is a "screen" — white+blue double bezel, Press Start 2P labels, blinking ► cursor on the leader.

DIRECTION 02

Saturday Teletext

The site as a broadcast text service — Ceefax crossed with the ESPN BottomLine. Pure black, phosphor amber/green/cyan, one mono font at two sizes, standings as agate score tables, the map drawn in block mosaics. Data-dense and fast, like checking scores on channel 888 in 1983.

Signature: fastext color-key navigation (red/green/yellow/cyan) and numbered pages — MAP P100, STANDINGS P200, SCORES P300.

DIRECTION 03

The Conquest Ledger

Leans into the imperialism instead of the arcade: a war-room campaign map. Khaki field paper, stencil headlines, typewriter dispatches, grease-pencil red for every territorial change. The weekly update reads like a wire telegram from the front. Zero glow, zero pixels — all ink.

Signature: conquests announced as typewritten FLASH dispatches with rotated CAPTURED stamps; the week selector is a manila file-tab row.

DIRECTION 04

Gridiron GeoCities

The 1996 fan-shrine your score marquee already wants to live on. Silver page on a starfield, outset bevel buttons, rainbow gradient headline, a real hit counter, a web ring in the footer. Deliberately kitsch — the joke is the point, and the data tables underneath it stay honest.

Signature: live hit counter + web ring footer + "best viewed in 800×600" — artifacts nobody ships anymore.
